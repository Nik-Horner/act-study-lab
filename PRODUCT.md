# ACT Study Lab

A student preparing for the enhanced digital ACT can study English, Math, Reading, and Science from one website and one existing Supabase account. English Lab is the incumbent product; this work extends its routes, account model, and visual identity rather than replacing them.

The immediate user is retaking the ACT on October 17, aiming for 36 in Math and Science, after a 34 Math and 27 Science. Available study time is approximately 60–90 minutes per day. Those are the user's goals, not defaults to assign to every account. Profiles supply each student's target and test date. New-subject baseline scores are unset unless actually supported by stored account data.

The primary scene is an evening study session on a computer: identify today's focused work, study a lesson, answer questions with evidence, review mistakes, and rehearse a timed section. Phone layouts support studying and review; full screen-based test rehearsal is best practiced on a computer and with ACT's official interface.

Success means the existing sign-in and English progress keep working, added subjects are findable from the dashboard, completed work persists through the existing database, original Reading content has substantive lessons and full-length passages, and missed tasks remain visible within the preparation plan's budget.

The daily session uses saved 60/75/90-minute settings. Format 5 schedules two bounded teaching scopes per subject, each followed by questions explicitly matched to its taught prerequisites in `src/lib/daily-curriculum.ts`. Later scopes continue the full topic. Concepts advance sequentially and save before the matching drill; both units precede fresh cumulative questions and a mandatory recap, then the next subject. Weak scopes return precisely, with previously answered questions deprioritized where the eligible pool permits. Full topic lessons and timed sections remain accessible through Extra practice.

Allocation first covers estimated teaching at 180 words per minute, example interactions, unique Reading passages at 220 words per minute, answer time, corrections, and recap. Remaining time follows score needs; additional matched questions enter only when the estimate fits. These are planning assumptions, not promised completion speeds. Reading's daily scopes retain one complete worked example per section; full topic access retains every original example. All subjects offer retrieval from memory. English complete-sentence classification is limited to determinate clauses and fragments; other examples are labeled comparisons. Reading worked examples reveal a supported answer.

Answers save before correction appears. Corrections retain the passage, table, or graph, show the selected and correct choices, and explain concrete steps. Saved history uses the same correction view. Question pacing targets are English 42 seconds, Math 67, Reading 67, and Science 60. Answer duration freezes before asynchronous saving; a failed save retries the same answer and duration. The account stores concept progress, answers, durations, review checks, the shared daily clock, and carryover. Historical answered question IDs, plan ID, and deadline survive teaching-format upgrades. Unfinished work consumes the next available daily budget instead of stacking another full session.

Math and Science practice can open the ACT-specific hosted Desmos graphing calculator. Science labels it a learning tool: ACT Science is calculator-free, and its timed exam does not offer the calculator. Full-section exam timers remain device-scoped with fixed deadlines; completed history, previous section scores, and daily settings are account-scoped.

Truth boundaries: original questions are independent practice. Neither raw accuracy nor pacing is a validated official scaled-score prediction, and the product does not guarantee a 36. Science is optional under the enhanced ACT. Local isolated-account QA and automated checks support the current implementation; exhaustive human semantic validation and production deployment are not established by this documentation. No Canvas artifacts.
