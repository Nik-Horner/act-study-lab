# ACT Study Lab

A student preparing for the enhanced digital ACT can study English, Math, Reading, and Science from one website and one existing Supabase account. English Lab is the incumbent product; this work extends its routes, account model, and visual identity rather than replacing them.

The immediate user is retaking the ACT on October 17, aiming for 36 in Math and Science, after a 34 Math and 27 Science. Available study time is approximately 60–90 minutes per day. Those are the user's goals, not defaults to assign to every account. Profiles supply each student's target and test date. New-subject baseline scores are unset unless actually supported by stored account data.

The primary scene is an evening study session on a computer: identify today's focused work, study a lesson, answer questions with evidence, review mistakes, and rehearse a timed section. Phone layouts support studying and review; full screen-based test rehearsal is best practiced on a computer and with ACT's official interface.

Success means the existing sign-in and English progress keep working, added subjects are findable from the dashboard, completed work persists through the existing database, original Reading content has substantive lessons and full-length passages, and missed tasks remain visible within the preparation plan's budget.

The daily session uses saved 60/75/90-minute settings. Format 6 presents four subject sections after login and prefers two lesson/drill units per subject. It defers an entire second unit when complete teaching and practice would exceed the budget; per-subject curriculum cursors keep deferred work from being skipped. Non-English questions explicitly match authored teaching scopes in `src/lib/daily-curriculum.ts`. Lessons precede their matching drills, followed by cumulative questions and mandatory recap before the next subject. Weak scopes return precisely, with previously answered questions deprioritized where the eligible pool permits. Full topic lessons and timed sections remain accessible through Extra practice.

Allocation first covers estimated teaching at 180 words per minute, example interactions, unique Reading passages at 220 words per minute, answer time, corrections, and recap. Remaining time follows score needs; additional matched questions enter only when the estimate fits. These are planning assumptions, not promised completion speeds. Reading's daily scopes retain one complete worked example per section; full topic access retains every original example. Reading, Math, and Science retain retrieval from memory; Reading worked examples reveal a supported answer. English preserves the original full topic lesson, labeled examples, QuickCheck, and original drill UI instead of replacing those examples with classification quizzes. Its introductory “Clauses and phrases” unit uses only six separately authored recognition questions; “Sentence fragments” uses only six fragment-repair questions. Clause examples are neutral in daily and standalone teaching. Original sentence-structure punctuation questions follow the complete original topic lesson and QuickCheck; other English topics likewise use complete original lessons and original question banks.

The daily English adapter reuses the original `DrillClient` rendering, 42-second pacing, and plain original explanations, with account saving before Next becomes available; standalone mastery behavior is unchanged. Other-subject corrections retain the passage, table, or graph, show the selected and correct choices, and explain concrete steps. Saved history retains question evidence and answer explanations. Question pacing targets are English 42 seconds, Math 67, Reading 67, and Science 60. Answer duration freezes before asynchronous saving; a failed save retries the same answer and duration. The account stores concept progress, answers, durations, review checks, the shared daily clock, and carryover. The format-5-to-6 migration retains non-English work, answers, durations, and clock. Required non-English prerequisite repairs append concepts without shifting prior concept keys and gate unanswered drills until taught. After content refresh, budget fitting estimates remaining unanswered drills, reviews, and unread practice passages while conservatively counting full teaching; it redistributes available slack, trims only unanswered extra drills while retaining one drill per unit and mandatory review, and defers untouched trailing units with cursor rollback when necessary; existing answers, history, and clock remain intact. New English practice is trimmed and whole units deferred against saved capacity, with history retained separately and the next cursor following actual retained units. Only when an older allocation cannot fit a complete original topic does migration use a short foundation refresher, leaving that topic next in the curriculum. Historical answered English questions remain preserved while corrected prerequisites are taught again. Plan identity and deadline survive upgrades. Unfinished work consumes the next available daily budget instead of stacking another full session.

Math and Science practice can open the ACT-specific hosted Desmos graphing calculator. Science labels it a learning tool: ACT Science is calculator-free, and its timed exam does not offer the calculator. Full-section exam timers remain device-scoped with fixed deadlines; completed history, previous section scores, and daily settings are account-scoped.

Truth boundaries: original questions are independent practice. Neither raw accuracy nor pacing is a validated official scaled-score prediction, and the product does not guarantee a 36.
